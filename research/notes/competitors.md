# Subtopic 2 — Competitor camps & what parents need

**Method and limits.** 6 searches. Page fetches were blocked, so these are search-engine extracts. Prices are third-party unless stated otherwise.

## Q1. What do comparable camps put in front of parents?

**Takeaway:** Every serious camp leads with *who / when / how much / is it safe*. Parents mostly decide on logistics. Safety information has to be there and be easy to find, but it rarely sells the camp on its own.

**Cited findings**
- **U.S. Space Camp, Huntsville.** Programs are segmented by age (Space Camp 9–11, Space Academy 12–14), at about $1,799–$1,899 per week per directory listings ([summer-camp-planner](https://summer-camp-planner.com/camps/us/al/huntsville/space-camp)). Six-day sessions are listed at $1,799–$1,999 for 2026 ([Kidvoyage](https://kidvoyage.com/camps/usa/space-camp-advanced-space-academy)). Lodging is included for programs of 3+ days ([findingtheuniverse](https://www.findingtheuniverse.com/space-camp-usa-Huntsville-Alabama/)). Travel is excluded from scholarship awards ([reviewr](https://my.reviewr.com/site/Space_Camp_General_Scholarship26)).
- **Huntsville publishes supervision ratios.** Trainee-to-team-leader is no more than 16:1, with a separate day and night team leader per team ([Rocket Center QA doc](https://www.rocketcenter.com/docs/SpaceCampQA.pdf)). The document is dated about 2019 per the extract.
- **NASA ASTRO CAMP Community Partners.** Youth organisations, schools, museums and libraries run NASA activities locally. Facilitators get a 1–2 day workshop and guides. 331 sites in FY2023, 31 outside the US ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-astro-camp-sets-new-record-while-providing-stem-opportunities), [Stennis page](https://www.nasa.gov/centers/stennis/education/students/astrocamp.html)).
- **ISRO YUVIKA (India, free).** Class 9 only, selection-based (Class 8 marks 50 %, quiz 10 %, activities). ISRO covers lodging, boarding and travel; 2026 ran May 11–22 ([space-india](https://space-india.com/isro-yuvika-2026/), [Inshorts](https://inshorts.com/en/news/isro-opens-yuvika-2026-applications-for-free-two-week-space-camp-1771910956493)). This is our reference point: parents compare a paid camp against a free but highly selective one.
- **IIT Bombay SpaceTech Summer School 2026.** ₹60,000 + GST plus ₹25,000 hostel, aimed at older students and professionals ([STAR Lab IIT-B](https://www.star-iitb.in/events/spacetech-2026)).
- **Parents' decision drivers.** A University of Michigan national poll found parents focus mostly on logistics. Fewer than half rate general safety policies as essential, and only half feel very confident they can judge whether a camp is safe ([Michigan Medicine](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/safety-isnt-always-top-mind-parents-when-choosing-summer-camps)). Checklists recommend asking about staff recruitment and training, emergency preparedness and director access ([UT Youth Protection](https://youthprotectionprogram.utexas.edu/2021/07/21/questions-to-ask-when-choosing-a-summer-camp/)).

## Q2. What do parents ignore?

**Inferences (mine)**
- Long philosophy paragraphs and generic "inspire the next generation" copy. None of the camp listings above lead with them.
- Galleries without captions. Photos only build trust when they show *where the child sleeps, eats and works*.

## Implications for our site
1. Dates, grades, venue and price state appear above the fold. Price is currently TBD, so the site says so plainly.
2. A **"Ground Station" section answers the parent checklist** in scannable form: supervision, medical, food, phones, travel, security. Where we don't know a number (e.g. our supervision ratio), the FAQ says "to be confirmed" and offers WhatsApp. **We do not invent a ratio.**
3. Position against YUVIKA honestly. It is free but selective and Class 9 only. We are open to Grades 7–12, residential, and hands-on every day.
